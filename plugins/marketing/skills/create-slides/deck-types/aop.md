# AOP (Annual Operating Plan)

A deck that builds and defends the case for a marketing investment portfolio for the coming year — presented to leadership/finance for budget approval. The core move is *allocates and defends*: not just stating a number, but building the strategic case for why the investment is sized and structured that way, tied to measurable revenue outcomes.

**Default shape:**

1. **Prior-year recap** — what was invested, what it returned, framed as the evidence base for this year's ask (not a victory lap or an apology — just the data the plan is built on).
2. **Market/situational context** — what's changed (competitive, category, regional) that the plan needs to account for.
3. **Strategic priorities** — the handful of bets the plan is organized around, stated before the numbers so the budget reads as a consequence of strategy, not a spreadsheet.
4. **Investment allocation** — the portfolio broken out by channel/division/region as relevant, each piece connected back to a priority above. Frame as a portfolio with tradeoffs, not a list of line items.
5. **Expected revenue outcomes** — for each major allocation, the measurable outcome it's expected to drive. This is the accountability anchor the whole deck builds toward — don't let spend be justified by activity alone.
6. **Risks/tradeoffs** — what was deprioritized and why, and what happens if the ask isn't fully funded.
7. **The ask** — the specific approval being requested, stated plainly as its own slide.

Tone: see [core:audience-archetypes](../../../../core/skills/audience-archetypes/SKILL.md)'s Executive Team persona — concise, numbers-forward, strategic rationale before detail.

Output the same plain-markdown deck format as any other deck — see the parent [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md).

*Note: this shape is drafted from general AOP planning conventions rather than a large sample of past decks — tighten it against real examples as they come through.*
