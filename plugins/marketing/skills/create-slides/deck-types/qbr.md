# QBR / exec business review

A quarterly business review for a C-suite/exec audience — marketing reporting on the quarter, but framed as a partner to the business, not just an activity report.

**Default shape** (adjust to what the content actually supports):

1. **Market/context overview** — what environment the quarter played out in.
2. **Results vs. plan (AOP)** — the numbers, but always with the "why" behind a miss or a beat, not just the delta. If there are multiple divisions/business lines, call out each rather than blending into one company-wide number.
3. **Forward plan for next quarter** — the specific levers being pulled, tied back to what part 2 showed.
4. **The ask** — budget, headcount, or a decision needed — as its own clearly-labeled slide near the end, not buried inside the forward plan.

Tone: see [core:audience-archetypes](../../../../core/skills/audience-archetypes/SKILL.md)'s Executive Team persona. Editorialize the numbers rather than just reporting them — for each section, lead with the so-what before the metric.

## Narrative restructuring

If the input is an existing QBR deck's content/outline rather than a topic to build from — the ask is to fix the story, not write new material:

1. Read the existing content in full before touching structure — identify what story it's currently telling (even unintentionally) and where it breaks: a slide in the wrong place, a summary that doesn't summarize, an ask that's missing or diluted, results with no "why."
2. Re-sequence and re-frame toward the default shape above, adjusted for whatever content actually exists — this is edit/reorder work, not a rewrite. Keep the user's actual content; move it, retitle it, tighten it, or split/merge slides as the flow requires.
3. Output the same plain-markdown deck format as any other deck (see the parent [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md)), but call out what changed and why in a short note above the deck (e.g. "moved the ask earlier — it was buried on slide 9; reframed the exec summary to lead with outcome, not activity").
