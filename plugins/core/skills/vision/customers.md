# Vision — Customers

Who Vision actually sells to and serves, sourced from lumenis.com (vision homepage, resource hub, patient/physician education, and product pages). This is about *market segments and channels*, not tone. For how to write to a given persona (KOL, Ophthalmologist, Optometrist, Practice Owner, Patient), see [audience-archetypes/vision-archetypes.md](../audience-archetypes/vision-archetypes.md); this file is the "who," that file is the "how to talk to them."

## Practice / channel types (the buyer)

- **Ophthalmology practices** — the primary buyer for glaucoma (SLT/YAG) and retina (photocoagulation) equipment; these are surgical/medical specialties, and Lumenis's physician-education content for this segment leans on peer-reviewed clinical evidence and CME-style webinars (named physician-presenters: Mark A. Latina MD, Robert Noecker MD MBA, Brian A. Francis MD MS) rather than consumer-style marketing. A regulatory note repeated in this content: "Federal (US) law restricts Lumenis devices to sale by or on the order of a licensed practitioner."
- **Optometry practices** — the primary buyer for dry eye equipment (OptiLIGHT, OptiPLUS); framed heavily around practice growth and ROI ("how I tripled my dry eye revenue," "$45,000 in the first 9 months"), a more overtly business-case-driven pitch than the ophthalmology/surgical content.
- **Dry-eye-focused boutique practices** — a distinct sub-channel: practices that build a specific service line or even a dedicated sub-brand around dry eye treatment (see the "boutique dry eye clinic," "boutique dry eye clinic and boutique optical" case studies in the resource hub) rather than offering it as one service among many.

## Patient segments (the end user)

- **Dry eye disease (DED/MGD) patients** — the largest, most heavily marketed patient segment. Includes sub-populations called out specifically in Lumenis's own content: contact-lens wearers (DED drives contact lens dropout), and patients recovering from LASIK/refractive or cataract surgery (perioperative dry eye).
- **Glaucoma patients** — specifically framed as people newly diagnosed or at risk: "three million Americans have it today but only half know they have it." The patient-facing pitch centers on an alternative to daily eye-drop compliance and the systemic side effects of medication, not on eye drops being ineffective outright — SLT is positioned as equivalent pressure control without the compliance burden.
- **Retinal disease patients** — the least consumer-marketed segment of the three; content is clinical/physician-facing rather than patient-facing, covering proliferative diabetic retinopathy, retinopathy of prematurity, retinal tears/detachment, branch retinal vein occlusion, choroidal neovascularization, and AMD.
- **Lower eyelid laxity patients** — OptiLIFT's audience; overlaps with the dry eye patient population (impaired blinking connects the two conditions) rather than being fully separate.
- **General public / consumer-awareness audience** — people who haven't yet connected their symptoms to a treatable condition, targeted through the celebrity-testimonial campaign (see [vision.md](vision.md)) rather than through a practice relationship at all; this is a demand-generation audience, not yet a patient of record anywhere.
