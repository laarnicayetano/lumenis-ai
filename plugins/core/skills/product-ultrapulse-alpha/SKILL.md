# Ultrapulse Alpha

Ultrapulse Alpha is Lumenis's next-generation CO2 laser device, launched in 2023. Lumenis commercialized the first CO2 laser device (ULTRAPulse) in 1990 — Ultrapulse Alpha continues that lineage (~34 years as of 2024), backed by 30+ years of clinical research on the underlying ablative CO2 category. (Previous version of this file said "60-year lineage" — that didn't match the 1990 origin date and has been corrected.)

## Target audience
- Hospitals and clinical practices, per FDA-approved indications.
- Marketed to "experts and beginners alike" — both experienced dermatologists/surgeons and newly trained practitioners.

## Talking points
- Next generation of a category Lumenis pioneered — the original ULTRAPulse CO2 laser (1990); widely referred to as the gold standard for CO2 ablative resurfacing.
- Efficient, effective, uniform delivery of energy.
- Ablation + coagulation MOA: 10,600 nm CO2, targeting water as the chromophore.
- WTF principle — wavelength, time, and fluence — governs energy delivery during a cell's photothermal relaxation time while avoiding skin damage.
- Cited proof points: 89% RealSelf "Worth It" rating, 30+ treatable indications, typical recovery ~1–2 weeks.

## Features
- MOA: ablation + coagulation; 10,600 nm CO2; high absorption of water (the target chromophore).
- Treatment applicators (fuller current lineup — this file previously listed only 4):
  - UltraScan — full and fractionated superficial resurfacing.
  - TrueSpot — 2.0mm collimated handpiece.
  - DeepFX Microscanner — deep fractional resurfacing.
  - SCAAR FX — deep fractional mode reaching up to 4mm in a single pulse (this file previously attributed the 4mm figure to DeepFX Microscanner instead — corrected here).
  - IncisionFX (0.2mm and 1.0mm) — incision/excision tool; used in blepharoplasty. (Previously called "Incision HP" in this file and linked to acne scars — Lumenis's site does not corroborate the acne-scar association; that use case is attributed to DeepFX/SCAAR FX instead. Flagging rather than asserting either way.)
  - Also referenced on Lumenis's site: BrushFX, LiteFX, GentleFX, ActiveFX, MaxFX, TotalFX, PigmentFX — full spec detail not yet pulled into this file.
