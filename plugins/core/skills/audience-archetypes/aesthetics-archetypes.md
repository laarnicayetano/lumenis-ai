# Aesthetics archetypes

## KOL (Presenting Physician)
An aesthetics physician presenting Lumenis technology at an event or training other aesthetic physicians — distinct from a physician evaluating the product purely as a customer.
- **Cares about**: being credible to a room of peers, having strong efficacy/safety data to stand behind, not overpromising cosmetic outcomes on the company's behalf.
- **Tone**: precise, evidence-led, peer-to-peer — not sales-y. Cite data instead of asserting claims.
- **Avoid**: marketing superlatives ("revolutionary," "game-changing") without data behind them; oversimplifying the science; putting words in their mouth.

## Physician
Dermatologist, plastic surgeon, or aesthetic physician evaluating or using Lumenis aesthetic devices clinically.
- **Cares about**: clinical outcomes, safety data, efficacy vs. alternatives already in their practice, patient downtime/satisfaction.
- **Tone**: precise, evidence-led, peer-to-peer.
- **Avoid**: marketing superlatives without data; oversimplifying the science.

## Practice Owner
Owns or runs the aesthetics practice/medspa deciding whether to buy or keep using Lumenis equipment.
- **Cares about**: ROI, patient throughput, staff training burden, financing, differentiation in a consumer-driven, often cash-pay market.
- **Tone**: business-minded, concrete numbers over adjectives, respectful of their time.
- **Avoid**: pure clinical jargon with no business translation; vague value claims without a way to estimate return.

## Patient
The aesthetics patient/consumer (patient education material, consumer-facing event content).
- **Cares about**: cosmetic outcome, safety, what the experience/downtime is like, why they should trust it.
- **Tone**: plain language, reassuring, benefit-first — no jargon, no internal product codenames.
- **Avoid**: clinical/technical language, internal metrics, anything that reads like it's written for a doctor instead of them.
