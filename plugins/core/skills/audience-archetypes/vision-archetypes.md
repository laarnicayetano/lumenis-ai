# Vision archetypes

## KOL (Presenting Physician)
A vision physician (ophthalmologist or optometrist) presenting Lumenis technology at an event or training other eye-care physicians — distinct from a physician evaluating the product purely as a customer.
- **Cares about**: being credible to a room of clinical/surgical peers, having strong data to stand behind, not overpromising on the company's behalf.
- **Tone**: precise, clinically rigorous, peer-to-peer — not sales-y. Cite data instead of asserting claims.
- **Avoid**: marketing superlatives ("revolutionary," "game-changing") without data behind them; oversimplifying surgical/clinical nuance; putting words in their mouth.
- **In correspondence** (email/one-to-one, vs. a training deck): she actively tunes between "more fun/exciting" and "more professional" on the same message — the right register is warm and energetic but still credible, not fixed at one pole. Reference relationship history and who made the introduction rather than opening cold. For sensitive or personal moments (e.g. a condolence note), it needs to read personable and specific to the person — explicitly not generic or templated.

## Ophthalmologist
MD/surgeon with a broader, often surgical scope of practice.
- **Cares about**: surgical/procedural outcomes, precision, safety profile, fit into surgical workflow, comparison to other surgical or laser options they already use.
- **Tone**: precise, clinically rigorous, peer-to-peer — assume surgical fluency.
- **Avoid**: oversimplifying surgical/procedural nuance; treating them like a generalist.
- **In correspondence**: prefers a scannable, itemized recap of what was discussed with clear ownership of next steps over flowing prose. For a quick logistics ask, brevity and one explicit action item matter more than warmth.

## Optometrist
OD, typically primary/frontline eye care with a non-surgical scope, often in a comanagement or referral relationship with ophthalmologists.
- **Cares about**: what they can offer directly within their scope, patient screening/referral fit, comanagement workflow with surgeons, practice differentiation.
- **Tone**: practical, primary-care oriented — respect the boundary of their scope of practice; don't assume surgical authority.
- **Avoid**: language that assumes a surgical scope of practice; conflating their concerns with an ophthalmologist's.

## Practice Owner (Ophthalmologist-owned)
Surgical practice or ASC decision-maker considering larger capital equipment.
- **Cares about**: ROI tied to surgical volume, reimbursement/insurance codes, integration into OR/surgical scheduling, capital equipment financing.
- **Tone**: business-minded, informed by surgical-practice realities (reimbursement, OR time).
- **Avoid**: treating this like a small elective-market purchase — surgical equipment decisions carry different financial and regulatory weight.

## Practice Owner (Optometrist-owned)
Primary eye care practice, often with a retail/optical component, evaluating non-surgical-scope technology.
- **Cares about**: fit within non-surgical scope, retail/optical revenue synergy, referral network value, budget relative to a typically smaller practice.
- **Tone**: business-minded, budget-conscious.
- **Avoid**: assuming surgical-practice economics or scope; pitching equipment outside what an OD-owned practice can use.

## Patient
The vision patient/consumer.
- **Cares about**: eye health and safety, vision outcome, trust — often a higher-stakes, less discretionary decision than an aesthetics purchase.
- **Tone**: plain language, reassuring, benefit-first — no jargon.
- **Avoid**: clinical/technical language; treating an eye-health decision with the same casualness as an elective cosmetic one.
