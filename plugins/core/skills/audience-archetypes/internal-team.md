# Internal team archetypes

## Sales / Channel Partner
Internal reps or distributors who need to represent the product to others.
- **Cares about**: clear talking points, competitive positioning, objection handling, what makes this product easy to sell.
- **Tone**: direct, scannable, arms them to repeat it confidently to someone else.
- **Avoid**: content that only makes sense to someone who already knows the product deeply — this audience is relaying it forward.
- **In correspondence**: wants a clear action list with a named owner per item, and for broader team-facing updates (e.g. a field-sales call or post), a connective narrative that strings separate wins into one story rather than a list of updates. Written as a peer/enabler to the field, not as their manager — explicitly avoid preachy or top-down phrasing. Keep coordination emails tight: cut redundancy and excess exclamation points rather than defaulting to high energy.

## Executive Team
Internal strategic/business-case correspondence to leadership — evaluating strategy, resourcing, and performance (distinct from HR-sensitive personnel correspondence, see below).
- **Cares about**: market opportunity, growth trajectory, strategic rationale, risk — and in practice, the business case stated plainly: constraints, ROI/revenue rationale, and a clear accountability structure. The specific decision or sign-off being requested should be named explicitly, not implied.
- **Tone**: concise, big-picture first with detail available on request, framed around business impact — direct, structured, and data-anchored, closer to a decision memo than a relationship email. Gets fewer edit rounds and less warmth-softening than KOL/physician correspondence.
- **Avoid**: deep technical/clinical detail unless it directly supports a strategic point; vague value claims without numbers; burying the ask.

## Internal HR-Sensitive Correspondence
Departures, compensation, and conflict/de-escalation messages involving the internal team — distinct enough in register from general Executive Team strategy content to warrant its own entry; this is the most heavily iterated tone in day-to-day internal writing.
- **Cares about**: avoiding language that creates HR or legal exposure (no unearned commitments, no accusatory phrasing even when the content is corrective), stating continuity/next-steps concretely so a team doesn't read instability into a departure or reorg, and asserting authority appropriately when addressing conflict — without it reading as either an overreach or a retreat.
- **Tone**: constantly balanced between stern/direct and warm/human depending on the specific stakes — a compensation or escalation matter reads leaner and more formal; a well-liked departure reads warmer; conflict resolution stays firm but not accusatory. Rejects anything that reads generically corporate or "too AI" in favor of a voice that still sounds personally written.
- **Avoid**: unearned commitments or promises that create HR exposure; accusatory language; sounding shaky or uncertain about team stability; generic/templated phrasing in emotionally loaded moments.
